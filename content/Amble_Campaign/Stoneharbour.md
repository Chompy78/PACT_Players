# Stoneharbour

![[arc02_among_strangers_banner.webp|Stoneharbour|750]]

A prosperous working port of three or four thousand people, on a coast none of the party had ever seen before they were sent there. Amble is a village of three hundred where everyone knows everyone; Stoneharbour is ten times that, and knows none of them.

It runs on the tide and on other people's cargo. Grain, salt, wool and fish come off the boats and go into warehouses; rope and barrels and sailcloth get made and sold back to the ships. The Iron Coin Guild keeps a branch here — a converted counting-house in the Guild Quarter — with the indentures' barracks on its Warehouse Row edge.

## The districts

### Harbourfront

The working waterfront: berths, boats, and everything that keeps them moving.

- **The Harbourmaster's Office** — assigns berths, logs arrivals and departures, settles captains' disputes over dock space
- **The Keel Yard** — a shipwright's yard, two slipways and a dry-dock cradle; repairs more hulls than it builds
- **Canvas & Batten** — a sailmaker's loft above the Keel Yard, its floor permanently pinned with half-finished sails
- **The Binnacle** — a ship's chandlery: rope, pitch, lanterns, charts, ship's biscuit
- **The Herring Hall** — the wholesale fish market, the night's catch auctioned at dawn in bulk lots
- **The Narrows Ferry** — a flat-bottomed ferry across the harbour mouth, a copper each way
- **The Steam Row** — a bathhouse, a copper for the shared tubs
- **The Harbour Exchange** — a covered exchange hall, a market of a different order to the Steps
- **The Brass Tide** — a tavern

### Warehouse Row

Storage, weighing, and the trades that serve them. The barracks sit on its Guild Quarter edge.

- **Harrick's Grain Warehouse** — bulk grain under Guild-inspected seal
- **Coldern's Salthouse** — salt and preserved provisions, packed for long voyages and long winters
- **The Feather & Fleece Warehouse** — wool bales stacked to the rafters
- **The Customs House** — where incoming cargo is declared, tariffed and cleared
- **The Assay Office** — weighs and certifies coin, bullion, and disputed cargo weight
- **The Full Barrel** — a general chandlery: rope off the coil, lamp oil, nails, tar, cheap tools
- **The Anvil Yard** — blacksmith and farrier; cart horses shod, dock chain mended
- **Stave & Hoop** — a cooperage, barrels built to survive a sea voyage
- **The Tallow Lamp** — a tavern, on the Guild Quarter edge

### Guild Quarter

Paperwork, law, and the Guild branch itself.

- **The Bonded Hand** — a notary doing the paperwork the Guild doesn't: wills, apprenticeships, private loans
- **Keys & Wardings** — locks, strongboxes and warded chests
- **The Fair Copy** — bookbinder and scribe; fair copies of contracts, ledgers and letters
- **The Petty Bench** — the Governor's petty court: small claims, market disputes, minor debts outside Guild contract law
- **Suds Row** — a laundry on the barracks edge, doing the indentures' linens and the Watch's uniforms

### Market Steps

The town's daily general market, laid out in tiers up a wide stair, with shops behind the stalls.

- **The Warm Crust** — a bakery older than the Guild branch; last loaves half-price at dusk
- **The Block** — the butcher; nothing wasted
- **Threadneedle** — tailor and clothier, mending cheap for regulars
- **Last & Awl** — a cobbler; busy every Marketday
- **Pressman's Corner** — a bookshop and lending library, the nearest thing the town has to a school
- **Dorn's Steady Hand** — the spell shop, upper tier
- **The Telling Stone** — a worn flat stone where a storyteller works most evenings for coin in a hat
- **The Painted Board** — a puppet-and-players' stage doing broad comedy and pointed satire
- **The Weathered Sign** — an inn

### Low Quay

The poor end of town, in the flood line. Where the least of the money reaches.

- **The Long Table** — a soup kitchen in a converted net-shed; one hot meal a day, no questions
- **The Cistern Steps** — the public well and washhouse, where most of Low Quay's actual gossip happens
- **Gull's Share** — a fishmonger working the cheap end of the day's catch
- **The Weighing Hook** — a pawnbroker, a wall of unclaimed nets and kettles and wedding bands
- **The Drip** — a flophouse, a penny a night, named honestly
- **The Render House** — renders hides, fat and bone for tallow and candle-stock; kept deliberately downwind
- **The Low Tide** — a tavern · **Gullyard Rooms** — a doss-house above a chandlery

### Ropewalks

Named for the long straight tracks where cordage is laid.

- **The Long Walk** — a cordage works running the district's namesake track
- **Salt & Sundry** — a secondhand shop: furniture, tools, jewellery
- **Ink & Iron** — a tattoo and branding parlour, licensed for trade-guild marks, ship's tattoos and mourning ink

### Shrine Hill

Above the town, quieter than the rest of it.

- **The Resting Stair** — an almshouse and hospice for those with nowhere else, a dozen cots and a kitchen
- **The Quiet Ground** — the burial ground, terraced into the hillside below the shrine
- **The Wayside Stone** — a worn standing stone off the main path, a minor wayside shrine

### Old Stone

The oldest quarter. Nobody has had reason to take you there yet.

## Who runs what

- **Governor Petra Whitlock** — the town's civil authority; the customs dues and the petty court are hers
- **Captain Halvard Stroud** — the Watch
- **Hesper Tuck** — head of the Harbour Concord, the working waterfront's own association
- **Selwyn Aske** — shrine-keeper, Shrine Hill
- **The Iron Coin Guild, Stoneharbour Branch** — a separate power to all of the above, and the one the party answers to
