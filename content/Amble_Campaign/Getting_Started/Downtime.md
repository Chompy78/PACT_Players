Time passes between chapters, sometimes a few weeks and sometimes more than a year. Most of it your character spends working for the Guild. **Downtime is the rest: your Restdays and evenings.** You choose what your character does with them.

## How it works

- **You get one pick each chapter.** A pick is one thing your character spends their spare time on.
- **We do picks at the start of the session**, before the first scene, all together. It takes about ten minutes. Come with an idea of what you want.
- **Missed the last chapter?** You get one extra pick, because your character was off doing something else.
- **Can't make the session?** Message your pick to the GM beforehand, and it's sorted out at the table without you.

## Safe or risky

For most picks you choose how hard to try. (A few, like training, always come down to luck.)

- **Steady** is the safe choice. You get a sure, modest result, with no luck involved.
- **Push** means you take your chances. If it goes well you get about twice as much. If it goes badly you get *less* than Steady would have given you, and something goes wrong.

Push when you're good at the thing. If you're not, Steady is usually the better bet.

## What you can do

### Earn some coin

Odd jobs on your Restdays: a cookshop on market nights, the fish market, carting, mending. It's a few gold for the season, more if you Push. As the years go on and you get better known, the pay goes up.

In Stoneharbour you also choose what happens to the money:
- **Declared:** it goes on your Guild debt. Safe.
- **Retained:** the Guild lets you keep a little of it, for a small fee.
- **Undeclared:** you keep all of it and don't tell the Guild. They might find out. You can tell the GM this one privately.

### Win over a crowd

Choose one part of town or one kind of people: the fish-quay families of Low Quay, the Market Steps stallholders, the
Watch, the merchants. Then spend your spare time where they'll see you: lend a hand when they're short, turn up when it
matters to them, put a little money into their causes. If it works, that crowd starts to think better of you.

- **It costs you something:** a few gold, and usually some unpaid work as well.
- **People who don't know you yet are easy to win over.** People who already dislike you take a Push, and the more they
  dislike you, the harder it is.
- **A crowd only warms to you so fast:** one improvement per chapter, however many of you try.
- **Do it with a friend** and you share the cost. If you both Push, you have a better chance together.

### Spend time with someone

Time with one person because you like them: a cup of tea, a hand with their work, an evening together. It's free.

- They won't think worse of you this time round, and they'll usually think better of you.
- If they already feel strongly about you, good or bad, moving them takes a Push.
- **It's not a favour-trade.** If you want something from them, that's *Look into*.

### Look into something

Ask around about something you've heard: a rumour, a person, a place. This one **always comes down to luck**. You might find nothing, or hear what the street says, or pick up a real lead. At the very top, the GM gives you a private hint about what's coming.

Asking too clumsily can mean people notice you asking.

### Put in for the Guild

Volunteer for extra contract days and the jobs nobody wants. It takes a little more off your debt. Push, and the Guild
might notice: it goes in your file, and that counts when promotion comes round.

### Work an angle

Grey or shady work for the people the Guild doesn't like you mixing with. This one **always comes down to luck**.

- If it goes well, you get coin nobody knows about, and the people you worked for like you more.
- Every job is at someone's expense. **Other groups may think worse of you**, and the worse it goes, the more people you annoy.
- If it goes badly, you might be caught.
- You can tell the GM this one privately.

### Quiet help

Give your spare time to people who need it, with your name left off it: a soup kitchen, a sickbed, a grave that needs digging, a schoolroom.

- You get no coin, and nobody thinks better of you for it. That's the point.
- Luck decides what comes of it. It's usually something small and good, like a useful bit of gossip or a steadier start to the next chapter. Sometimes you come back tired or under the weather.

### Train

Learn something new with a teacher the Guild finds you. This one **always comes down to luck**. See the next section.

### A longer project

Something that takes several chapters: clearing a mark from your Guild file, looking after someone, a public good deed with the whole party, and in Fordwater a hidden room or a small trade of your own. Ask the GM.
The Landing deed in Stoneharbour has to be done in a single break between chapters.

## Training, explained

Training makes something you want to buy with AP cheaper.

- **Pick the thing you're training toward**: a skill, a fighting move, a spell, more hit points, anything you'd buy
  with AP. The Guild finds you a teacher.
- **Training always comes down to luck**, helped by whatever fits: the skill itself, the tool, Athletics for fighting
  moves, Arcana, Religion or Nature for magic, Constitution for toughness, and so on. The GM will tell you which.
  - **If it goes well** you get **Bonus AP** toward that thing: 1, or 2 if it goes really well.
  - **If it goes so-so** you get nothing this time.
  - **If it goes badly** you hurt yourself in practice. The Guild medic's bill goes on your debt.
- **The Bonus AP can only be spent on that thing.** Training picks add up, and together they can pay for up to half of
  it. You buy it as normal when you're ready.
- **Learning something brand new** (a first cantrip, a skill you've never had) is harder, because you're starting with
  nothing to build on.
- **The gold cost is the same as always**, with the Guild paying half.

*Example: Archer wants to open a new kind of magic, which costs 7 AP. He trains toward it and it goes well, so he gets 1
Bonus AP. When he buys it, he pays the other 6 AP and his half of the gold.*

## Working together

Two of you on the same thing (the same group, the same person or the same question) usually helps. Ask the GM how.
