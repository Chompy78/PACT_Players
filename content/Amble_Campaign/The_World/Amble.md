Amble is a small farming village of around three hundred people — the kind of place where everyone knows everyone, and a stranger is noticed before they've finished tying up their horse. The wider kingdom Amble belongs to feels far away most days; what's close is the stream, the fields, and whoever you grew up next to.

## Geography

Amble sits along the north road, where it crosses a stream — a bridge and a mill mark the spot. The village square is the true centre of things, with the tavern and inn close by, the shrine a short walk off, and houses and workshops scattered around them. The smithy and tannery sit a little further out, doing the loud and the smelly work respectively. Cart-tracks and footpaths run out from the square toward the crossroads and the farmland beyond.

Winters here are mild and summers are warm — good weather for steady farming, not the kind of place where the land itself fights you.

## Landmarks

### The Council Tree

An old oak standing near the village square — Amble's real seat of government, such as it is. There's no hall, no chair of office, just a big tree and whoever's willing to sit under it. The village's elders gather here to hear disputes and settle village business, in full view of anyone who wanders past. Old Marn's cottage sits nearby.

### The Cubby Tree

A separate tree out toward the fields on the village's edge, near the grazing paddocks and the granary — a working edge of the village, not a scenic one. This is where Amble's children build their own den, away from the grown-ups' business — theirs to meet in, theirs to keep secrets in. Kids tie a scrap of cloth to it when they want a wish; the grown-ups pretend not to notice it's still there.

### Elsewhere in Amble

- **The village square** — where market days happen and news travels fastest.
- **The tavern and inn.**
- **The shrine** — home to Mother Yela, and to the bell loft in its tower, which [[Arc01_prelude/NPCs/index|Wren]] has claimed as her own space.
- **The Woodline** — the woods at the village's edge, where the fields give way to trees. Children swap stories about what lives out there past the first twenty paces; not all of those stories turn out to be just stories.
- **The cemetery** — set apart from the day-to-day bustle. Amble meets death calmly here, without spectacle.
- **The smithy and the tannery** — the village's noisiest and smelliest trades, both a little apart from everything else for good reason.
- **The stream, the bridge, and the mill** — where the north road crosses the water, and where most of Amble's cart traffic funnels through.

## Custom and Manner

Amble is grounded and low-key — the kind of place with no prophecy, no cult, and no dark secret behind the neighborly small talk. Just people who've lived close together a long time.

**Greetings and everyday manners**
Villagers greet each other by name and trade or family — "Morning, Tanner," "Morning, Coll's girl" — not by title, and returning the greeting is basic courtesy. A raised hand and a nod across a field counts as a full greeting; stopping to talk is optional. Strangers get a plain, neutral "Morning" and a look — friendly enough, but the villager keeps working and waits to see what the stranger wants. Obvious travelers get a practical question instead of a welcome — "Where you headed?" — because placing you on the map matters more than pleasantries. Children are expected to greet elders first; forgetting earns a mild, public correction, not shame.

**Hospitality**
A traveler caught by nightfall is offered a roof as a matter of course — the shrine keeper takes in anyone the households can't. People say someone is "under the roof" rather than "taken in," which makes it communal rather than pitiable. Hosts give what they have without ceremony; making a fuss over a guest is faintly suspect — you feed them like family, not like a lord. A guest is expected to offer something back — a hand at chores, news from the road, a coin only if they clearly have one to spare. Overstaying without pulling your weight sours a welcome fast, and turning away a hungry person is genuinely shameful here.

**Strangers and outsiders**
The default is wary-but-decent: not frightened, not effusive — Amble watches a newcomer a day or two before it warms up. Repeat traders are treated half like locals; a new one is judged on fair dealing, fast. Officials — tax collectors, Guild agents, anyone carrying the crown's authority — get correct, quiet, minimal cooperation and nothing volunteered; a border village learns early not to hand authority extra rope. Being frontier folk, Amble assumes trouble comes from outside, so a stranger's first job is proving they didn't bring any along.

**Settling disputes**
Small quarrels are expected to be settled between the people involved — running to the elders over nothing is childish. What can't be settled goes under the Council Tree, where a few respected elders hear both sides openly, in front of whoever's about; sunlight is the mechanism. Outcomes lean restorative, not punitive — repair the fence, replace the goat, apologise plainly, shake on it. Escalating a dispute past the village — to the crown or the Guild — is treated as a small betrayal of Amble itself, done only when there's truly no other road. Once the Tree has spoken, a matter is settled; nursing a grudge afterward is itself a minor disgrace.

**Market and trade**
Gentle haggling is expected and enjoyed — naming your first price too high or too low both mark you as a poor trader. Honesty about goods is close to sacred: hiding a flaw is the real sin, not driving a hard bargain. A deal is sealed by word and a handclasp, and going back on a handclasp deal is remembered for years. Credit and barter are normal between locals; demanding coin up front from a known villager is faintly insulting.

**Sayings you'll hear**
- *"Weather the edge, mind the middle."* — handle the frontier threat, but don't neglect the daily work.
- *"A handclasp holds longer than a hinge."* — your word outlasts anything you build.
- *"Feed the road and the road feeds you."* — kindness to travellers comes back around.
- *"Even the mill waits its turn at the stream."* — patience; everyone gets their water.
- *"Count your fences before you count your luck."* — do the work before you trust fortune.
- *"Quiet fields, full barns."* — no news is good news; boasting invites trouble.

**Small traditions**
The first loaf of the season is left on the Council Tree's roots "for the village" — really just a shared marker of the harvest, but no one skips it. Nobody whistles in the grain store; old belief says it "calls the mice," though it's mostly just habit now. A traveler given shelter is sent off with "road be short" — a plain blessing, no magic implied. Funerals are unhurried and held apart at the cemetery; the whole village walks, work stops, and death is met calmly, never with dread. A mild frost on the shrine's step is read as "a kind year coming" — cheerful nonsense everyone half-believes anyway.
