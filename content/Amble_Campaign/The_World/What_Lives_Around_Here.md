Stoneharbour is a working port. Most of what you meet has two legs and a grievance. This page is for the
rest.

Read the box your character qualifies for — see [[What_Your_Character_Knows|What Your Character Knows]].

> [!info]- ⚪ COMMON — no proficiency needed
> **Dogs** are everywhere and mostly harmless. Warehouse Row keeps mastiffs that are loud first and
> toothy second. A fighting dog is a different animal and you will know one when you see it.
>
> **Rats** live in the cisterns, the Render House and every cellar in Low Quay. One is nothing. A cellar
> full is a real problem, and people have died of the bites rather than the fight.
>
> **Wolves** are a winter problem, out past the fields. They are never alone, they take whoever is last,
> and they do not fight you if they can help it.
>
> **Boars** kill more country people than wolves do. Farmers name the bad ones.
>
> **Bears** live in the deep woods. A black bear leaves if you let it. A brown bear does not.
>
> **Goblins** are an up-country story here, not a Stoneharbour one. Raiders off the water are a real fear
> and have not happened in years.
>
> **The dead do not walk in Halden.** If you ever see one that does, that is not a monster, it is a
> disaster, and somebody caused it.
>
> The old rule everybody's grandmother says: *it is not the wolf you can see.*

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **Nature** and **Survival**
> Here is what things actually fight like, in the same advancement points your character is built from.
> Your party are around 100 each.

>
> A single wolf is not the problem. Five wolves and a leader are worth more than the five of you.

> [!warning]- 🟣 RARE — **expertise in both Nature and Survival**
> Everything gains by being stretched, the same way you do. A wolf that has hunted six winters is not the
> wolf born last spring, and the one that leads the pack has survived what killed the rest.
>
> You can read it before the fight starts. The old wolf hangs back and will not be baited. The goblin
> chief sends the others first. An ogre that has lived twenty years did it by being careful, not by being
> strong.
>
> So when something behaves oddly well, that is your warning, and it comes before anything bites. The
> creature that hesitates has done this before.
