Ask a grown-up in Amble what's past the crossroads and you'll get an actual answer, not just a story.

[[Amble|Amble]] belongs to the Kingdom of Halden, ruled from a capital called Aldermoot — a good many days' travel north of anything Amble folk would call nearby.

## The Crown

Halden is ruled by King Aldric Vane. Nobody in Amble has met him, and probably nobody ever will — but his face is on the coin, his law is what the village elders answer to if trouble ever climbs high enough to need it, and twice a year his tax collectors make the trip out to the South March to remind everyone he exists.

## The South March

Amble's own corner of the kingdom is called the South March — Halden's southern frontier, hillier and more forested than the soft farmland further north, and cooler under all those trees. The March has its own lord, whose job is watching the border and keeping the peace out here, since the crown trusts this land to someone who can actually defend it rather than just administer it.

## Stoneharbour

The nearest real town — reached from Amble by the river road — is Stoneharbour: a proper market town with a harbour on the sea, ships coming and going, and more strangers passing through in a week than Amble sees in a season. Anyone from Amble who's traveled at all has probably been to Stoneharbour and nowhere else.

## What Travelers Say About the Rest

Nobody in Amble has seen much past the March, but travelers talk, and their stories travel faster than they do.

- **South, over the mountains:** a hard, stone-grey country the traders call Kaldrun — cold, mineral-rich, and not somewhere you want your luck to run out. The one road through is watched closely on both sides, and what passes along it isn't always cargo.
- **West, past the treeline:** Aeloria, where — so the story goes — elves are held above everyone else by their own priests, and outsiders don't always find as warm a welcome as they would at home.

Nobody in Amble would call themselves well-traveled enough to confirm any of it. That's rather the point of a traveler's tale.
