Nobody in Amble owns an almanac, but everybody knows what month it is, because the month is what you are
doing. You can tell Lambmonth from Reapmonth by how tired the farmers look.

## The twelve months

Twelve months, three to a season, about thirty days each.

| Season | Month | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | **Sowmonth** | Snowmelt, mud, planting begins. The first shrine visit of the year for most households |
| Spring | **Lambmonth** | Livestock birthing. A hard, sleepless, un-devout month — what faith there is happens in a byre at three in the morning |
| Spring | **Bloommonth** | Late-spring flowering. Marriages and namings cluster here; children born over winter get named now rather than at birth |
| Summer | **Sunmonth** | Haymaking. Nothing formal happens all month because everybody is in a field |
| Summer | **Fairmonth** | The **Midsummer Fair** falls at the midpoint — civic, commercial, and about as devotional as a bake stall |
| Summer | **Reapmonth** | The first grain cut. The first of it is left standing or given away rather than sold: a small real cost, cheerfully paid |
| Autumn | **Harvestmonth** | Main harvest, apples in. The busiest shrine month of the year |
| Autumn | **Threshmonth** | Grain threshed, fruit pressed — and **debts and wages traditionally settled**, after the threshing |
| Autumn | **Leafall** | Short days. The dead are visited, graves tidied, names said aloud |
| Winter | **Saltmonth** | The pre-winter cull. Meat salted, food put by, and a share set aside for the almshouse or for whoever will not have enough |
| Winter | **Longmonth** | The **Midwinter Solstice dance** — *Longnight* — at the midpoint. Fires kept lit until morning. The most joyful night of the year |
| Winter | **Hungermonth** | The lean stretch before spring, when the stores run low |

Halden's winter is Saltmonth, Longmonth and Hungermonth. If you were expecting the cold at the other end of
the year, you are thinking of somewhere else.

## The week

Seven days. Most of them have no name worth using — people say First-day, Second-day and so on, and nobody
has ever needed better. Two are different:

- **Marketday** — the village's weekly trade day. Not the once-a-year Fair; just the day the carts come in.
- **Restday** — the last day of the week. A day of rest, and the day small accounts are not settled. See
  [[Shrines_And_Keepers#restday|Shrines and Keepers → Restday]], including the one institution in town
  that does not keep it.

## The year, and what year it is

About 365 days. No leap years, no almanac arguments — twelve months of thirty-odd days and then it starts
again.

Years are counted in the **Halden Reckoning**, written **H.R.**, from the founding of the kingdom. In
practice nobody says it out loud. A dated document says *the 14th of Saltmonth, 1167*, and the clerk who
wrote it would look at you oddly if you asked which reckoning.

That date is not a random example. **It is the day you walked out of Amble** under Guild escort, bound for
a coast none of you had seen.

Some anchors, if you want to work out how long you have been at this:

- The year in Amble — the broken charm, the Fair, the autumn argument, the winter that cost more than
  anyone meant to pay — ran through **1167**: Sowmonth, Fairmonth, Harvestmonth, and then Saltmonth.
- You left on the **14th of Saltmonth, 1167**.
- The walk up Shrine Hill was **Sowmonth, 1169** — about seventeen and a half months into service, and the
  mud was not dry yet.

So it is **1169**, and you have been the Guild's for the better part of two years.
