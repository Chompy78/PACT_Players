Everything on this page is what an ordinary person in Stoneharbour could find out by asking. For wages and
everyday goods, see [[Money_and_Prices|Money and Prices]].

**Three tiers of knowledge** — read the one your character has, see
[[What_Your_Character_Knows|What Your Character Knows]].

> [!info]- ⚪ COMMON — no proficiency needed
>
> ### Hiring somebody to do magic for you
>
> | | | |
> |---|---:|---|
> | Reading or identifying a thing you found | **5 GP** | Dorn's, an afternoon |
> | **A proving** — is there really anything in this, and is whoever made it still alive? | **5 GP** | **Ask for one before you buy anything** |
> | Closing a wound, one casting | **15 GP** | |
> | Lifting a disease or a poison that has taken hold | **40 GP** | |
> | Something a physician can name but not touch | **100 GP** | Usually a temple |
> | A binder's day, for work you commission | **15 GP** | Five of them in town |
> | Refilling a charm you have already spent | **15–20 GP** | **Keep the bead. It is not rubbish** |
>
> ### Household magic — what a comfortable house owns
>
> | | |
> |---|---:|
> | A needle that mends | **15 GP** |
> | A pot that boils | **18 GP** |
> | Clothes that mend their own tears | **20 GP** |
> | A lamp-bead | **20 GP** |
> | A hall lamp — light, at will, for good | **25 GP** |
> | A cold-box — food keeps | **45 GP** |
>
> **None of it needs attunement, none of it ever runs out, and none of it will help you in a fight.** A
> well-off family owns the pot. A rich one lights the hall and keeps the cold-box. **A docker owns none
> of it** — eighteen gold is three months of his wage.
>
> ### Charms — a spell in a bead, waiting
>
> | | | Uses |
> |---|---:|---|
> | **A potion of healing** | **50 GP** | one |
> | A knit — closes a wound | 70 GP | one |
> | A fall-charm — you land gently | 70 GP | one |
> | A proving-glass — shows you what is magical | 70 GP | one |
> | A tongue — you understand anything spoken | 70 GP | one |
> | **A step — you are 120 feet away, instantly** | **120 GP** | one |
> | A ward-bead — a blow that would have hit you does not | 120 GP | one |
> | A daily ward-bead | 350 GP | **once every day** |
> | A courier's stride | 350 GP | **three times a day** |
>
> **Charms with one use in them cost nothing to carry and need no attunement.** The ones that refill
> every day cost six times as much and **do** need a slot.
>
> ### Gear, and the things a party actually wants
>
> | | |
> |---|---:|
> | A sword that glows | 200 GP |
> | Rope that will not fray | 250 GP |
> | A rope of climbing | 500 GP |
> | A bag of holding | 740 GP, **and nobody is selling** |
> | **A +1 weapon** | **1,900 GP** |
> | **A +1 shield** | 1,900 GP |
> | **A Cloak of Protection** | **2,500 GP** |
> | A +2 anything | 4,250 GP, **and it needs stock that is not sold here** |
> | A +3 anything | about 6,800 GP, **and nobody will sell you one at any price** |
>
> **A +1 sword costs more than buying a person out of an indenture.** Everyone knows roughly what these
> cost. Almost nobody has ever held one.

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **Arcana** and **Investigation**
> **Every enchanted thing is priced by the breath in it** — the points somebody commits for as long as
> the thing exists — at about **55 gold a point**, plus the object itself.
>
> | The thing | Points |
> |---|---|
> | **Household magic** — below cantrip, no use in a fight | **none at all.** Materials and an evening |
> | A spell, **one use** | **spell level × 1** — and the points come back when it is spent |
> | A spell, **once a day** | **spell level × 3** — committed for good |
> | A spell, **three times a day** | **spell level × 6** |
> | A cantrip **at will** | 3 |
> | A tool with no spell in it | 4–20, by how much work it saves |
> | **Anything that puts a number on your sheet** | **30 / 60 / 120** for +1 / +2 / +3 |
>
> **So a step is 2 points** — a 2nd-level spell, used once — **and 120 gold.** A daily ward-bead is the
> same spell three times over, 6 points, 350 gold.
>
> **Having one made is about half the price of buying one**, if you supply the breath yourself. You pay
> the object, the materials and the binder's fee; you do not pay to rent a stranger's life. **What it
> costs you instead is that those points are committed for as long as the thing exists.**
>
> **And the choice the prices are really offering you:** a daily ward-bead and six one-use ward-beads
> both cost 350 gold. **Six beads are better value and worse in a crisis.**

> [!warning]- 🟣 RARE — **expertise in both Arcana and Investigation**
> **A spent bead is worth keeping.** Refilling costs the binder's fee alone — fifteen or twenty gold —
> because the points were never lost, only lent. **A party who keep their spent charms and find a
> friendly binder are buying them at a sixth of the price for the rest of their lives.**
>
> **A daily charm refills at dawn out of its maker's life, not yours.** So one whose maker has died
> **works once more and then never again** — which is the clearest sign this world gives that somebody
> is dead.
>
> **You can force a spent charm to work with your own breath**: one point per spell level, and you get
> it back at a point a day. You do not need the Art — **the charm already knows the shape of the spell
> and is only asking for a life to run it.** Two points and two days for a step. It is the emergency
> option, and it is how somebody with no gold gets one more try.
>
> **And the reason the expensive things stay expensive:** a maker can only hold up so much at once, and
> a +3 needs a component that is not for sale anywhere. **The gold is never what stops you.**
