Sooner or later you will be holding something you cannot explain.

Read the box your character qualifies for — see [[What_Your_Character_Knows|What Your Character Knows]].

> [!info]- ⚪ COMMON — no proficiency needed
> **The Weighing Hook, Low Quay.** A pawnbroker's shop with a wall of unclaimed nets, kettles and wedding
> bands. **Isley Grubb** quotes a fair price and a hard deadline in the same breath, and he means both.
>
> Pawn is a loan, not a sale. You get a fraction, you get a date, and past the date it is his.
>
> **Salt & Sundry, the Ropewalks**, buys secondhand outright: furniture, tools, jewellery. No questions
> beyond the ones that keep him out of trouble.
>
> **The Assay Office** weighs and certifies coin and bullion. Honest, official, and the last place to take
> anything you would rather nobody recorded.
>
> Everybody knows there is a trade in things that should not be sold. Nobody respectable knows where it is.

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **Sleight of Hand** and **Deception**
> **Expect a third of what a thing is worth**, less if it is identifiable. A plain tool sells. A named one
> does not.
>
> The questions you get asked are not about the object. They are about you: who you work for, who sent
> you, and whether anybody is going to come in here after you have gone.
>
> **A thing with a mark on it is a problem.** Guild property is stamped, a binder's work carries his mark,
> and both are traceable to a person who will be asked.
>
> The honest advice is that the first sale is easy and the second is how people get caught, because you go
> back to the man who was kind to you.

> [!warning]- 🟣 RARE — **expertise in both Sleight of Hand and Deception**
> None of this is free-standing. The trade in Stoneharbour is licensed, informally, by one person, and a
> cut goes upward whether you offered it or not.
>
> What that buys is real: the buyer stays open, the Watch stays uninterested, and a disagreement gets
> settled rather than reported.
>
> What it costs is that you are now known. Somebody keeps a list, and being on it is not the same as being
> in trouble until the day it is.
>
> The people who last in this trade sell rarely, sell plain things, and never explain themselves twice.
