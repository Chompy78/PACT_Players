Everybody in Low Quay knows the trade exists. Almost nobody can tell you anything true about it.

Read the box your character qualifies for — see [[What_Your_Character_Knows|What Your Character Knows]].

> [!info]- ⚪ COMMON — no proficiency needed
> A **sliver** is a piece of a life, cut out of somebody and sold.
>
> They are sold on the Low Quay, in taverns and back rooms, by men who do not look like anything. Nobody
> respectable admits to buying one. The people who sell them are people who ran out of other ideas.
>
> It is not illegal. That surprises people. There is no law in Halden against selling a piece of yourself,
> and no law against buying one, and everybody involved would rather you did not think about that too
> hard.
>
> The person afterwards is called **short**. They do not sicken and they do not die of it. They are just
> less than they were, for the rest of their life, and usually cannot say what is missing.
>
> What it does for the buyer, nobody will explain properly. It works. That is as much as the street will
> tell you, and the people who say more are usually selling.

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **Investigation** and **Insight**
> The money is the clearest thing about it. **A seller gets about fifty gold** for a piece of themselves,
> paid at once, no questions, no bond, no waiting.
>
> Compare that with the honest version of the same transaction — renting your life out to hold up
> somebody's enchanted property, which pays a dockhand five or ten gold a point if anybody will deal with
> him at all — and you have the whole reason the Quay is not short of sellers.
>
> **The trade is not chaotic. It is organised**, and it is tolerated rather than hunted. Somebody licenses
> it, somebody takes a cut, and a dealer who sells outside that arrangement does not sell for long.
>
> Buyers are not who you expect. Not desperate people — desperate people are the *sellers*. Buyers have
> money and a reason.

> [!warning]- 🟣 RARE — **expertise in both Investigation and Insight**
> Follow it up and it stops being a street trade. The men on the Quay are retail. There is a supplier
> behind them, and above the supplier there is an arrangement with somebody who could stop it and does not.
>
> The useful question is never who is selling. It is who is buying, in quantity, and what they are doing
> with it — because the retail trade cannot account for everything that is bought.
>
> And the part nobody says out loud: there is no record anywhere of a seller getting better. Not one. The
> trade is entirely in one direction, and everybody in it knows.
