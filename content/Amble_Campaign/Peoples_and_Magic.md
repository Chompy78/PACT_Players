Amble is a small village, but the wider world it sits in is not just one kind of people. None of this is exotic to a local — it's just what growing up here looks like.

## Who Lives Here?

**Humans** vary a lot by region — no single look, build, or manner covers all of them. Steadiness and keeping your word to household, trade, or village matter more than where you're from.

**Elves** are slightly taller and leaner than humans, weathered from outdoor work, with faintly pointed ears — dressed like their neighbors, not set apart. A 200-year-old elf looks about 60; there's no ageless glow here. Plain-spoken, unhurried, comfortable with long silences, and slow to forgive a broken promise — long lives make patience a real virtue and a betrayal something you'll remember for decades.

**Half-elves** often pass as human at a glance, with just a faintly pointed ear or a leaner build giving it away. Fast to read a room and match it, which can look like having no fixed way of being — but among people they've chosen, fiercely loyal. [[Arc01_prelude/NPCs/index|Marek Voss]] is one.

**Dwarves** stand around 4.5 feet, broadly built, with plain long hair and beards and practical trade clothes. Direct and dry without being unfriendly about it — bluntness reads as respect for your time here, not rudeness. Sound work and a kept word matter more than anything flashy.

**Halflings** stand around 3.5 feet, slim rather than round, in ordinary trades and ordinary clothes. Warm, quick talkers who wrap serious points in a joke or a story — easy to underestimate for it.

**Gnomes** stand around 3 feet, narrow, dressed soberly and practically. Precise and economical talkers — say the exact thing once, and long-winded people wear them out fast. Careful, accurate work is a point of quiet pride.

**Half-orcs** are larger and heavier-built than most humans, with slight tusks and muted skin tones. Straightforward and even in how they talk — many keep a deliberately level tone, tired of being read as a mood rather than a person.

**Orcs** are taller and heavier again than half-orcs, with pronounced tusks and a heavier brow, in muted greys, olives, and warm browns, wearing hard-wearing leather and pattern-woven wool. Value dignity, memory of place, and household loyalty; plain-spoken in private, more measured in public.

**Tieflings** have ordinary human proportions with small infernal traits — small horns (usually a couple of inches), a thin tail, sometimes unusual eyes — otherwise dressed and built like anyone else in their trade or station. Fully accepted here, and touching a tiefling's horns or tail without asking is simply rude, same as grabbing anyone else uninvited. [[Arc01_prelude/NPCs/index|Wren]] is one.

**Dragonborn** are roughly human height, with muted earthen scales, a short muzzle, and small ridges, usually in a heavy cloak against the weather. Rare everywhere, not just here — nobody has a homeland to point to, so they build ties one person at a time and hold onto them.

## How Common Is Magic?

Minor magic is an ordinary, unevenly-spread talent — like a good singing voice or a strong arm. Roughly three people in ten can manage at least a small cantrip, often untrained and a little informally, and most extended families have someone who can do a little something. A magical convenience — a pot that keeps food hot on its own, say — is a real luxury for those who can afford one.

Real spellcasting — a proper spellbook, real training behind it — is a different matter, but it starts out unremarkable too. A trained caster's early tricks are a genuine skill, worth paying someone for, but they don't turn heads — plenty of small towns have someone who can do this much. It's only once a caster's power grows serious that people actually start to talk. Above even that, a bare handful of names per nation carry power worth a ruler's or the Guild's attention. And at the very top, magic that could reshape a life in a single stroke is close to unheard of outside a few of the continent's oldest, longest-lived hands at their craft.

When spellwork is visible at all, it shows only in the moment of casting — a muted warm gold or a dull silver glow, nothing sharper. Enchanted things otherwise look like ordinary, well-made objects.
