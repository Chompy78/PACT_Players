1 gold (GP) = 10 silver (SP) = 100 copper (CP).

Most of Stoneharbour lives in coppers. If someone quotes you a price in gold, they are either selling
something serious or seeing how you react.

Read the box your character qualifies for — see [[What_Your_Character_Knows|What Your Character Knows]].

> **Healing is the expensive kind.** Closing a wound costs 15 GP. Lifting a disease is 40. A potion of
> healing is 50. See [[What_Magic_Costs|What Magic Costs]].
>
> A docker earning 2 SP a day would work 250 days for one potion. That is the arithmetic, and it is why
> people do desperate things when somebody they love is hurt.

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **Investigation** and **Insight**
> Your 25 GP a month is not what it looks like. A dockhand earns about 6 GP a month. You are credited at
> roughly five times that, and then charged your keep at a matching rate, which leaves about 2 GP.
>
> The Guild books your labour at a skilled rate and your bed and meals at one too. Side by side it looks
> generous. It nets to nothing, and it is meant to.
>
> Taking your 2 GP as a Personal Draw instead of putting it on the debt buys a hundred bowls at Dela's,
> or resoled boots and a fortnight of beer with change. It also costs you exactly one month of progress.
>
> A shopkeeper's margin is thinner than yours. A cookshop like Dela's grosses maybe 45 GP a month and
> keeps 3 to 5 once stock, fuel, rent and three people's keep are paid. In a bad month it keeps nothing.
> Everyone in the street calls that a successful business.

> [!warning]- 🟣 RARE — **expertise in both Investigation and Insight**
> Almost nobody in this town accumulates anything. The wages and the prices are set against each other so
> that an ordinary working life ends about where it began. Saving means going without, and going without
> is how people end up ill.
>
> Debt is the ordinary condition here, not a disgrace. Half of Low Quay owes somebody. The Guild is the
> largest and most patient creditor, and the only one that writes everything down.
>
> A windfall is dangerous. Twenty gold you cannot explain, with nowhere safe to keep it, makes you a great
> many new friends. Money in this town is mostly evidence.
