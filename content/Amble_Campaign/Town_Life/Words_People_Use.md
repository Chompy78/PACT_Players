Stoneharbour has its own words for things, and which one a person uses tells you where they drink. None
of it is secret, and all of it is worth knowing before somebody uses it at you.

**Three tiers of knowledge.** Read the one your character has — see
[[What_Your_Character_Knows|What Your Character Knows]].

> [!info]- ⚪ COMMON — no proficiency needed
> | Word | What it means |
> |---|---|
> | **stock** | what a person has made of themselves: everything they have learned and can do. *"Good stock in that one."* Politer people say **measure** |
> | **breath** | what a person can hold up in the world, mostly enchanted things. *"There's a breath of Dorn's in that lamp."* Politer people say **bearing** |
> | **a binder** | the trade that puts breath into objects. Five in a town this size, and all of them charge more than you think is reasonable |
> | **a brewer** | potions and draughts. Usually the apothecary, and usually somebody's aunt |
> | **a mark** | a binder's stamp on his own work. A good mark is worth more than the object under it |
> | **a proving** | having a second binder test a piece to see if there is anything really in it. An evening's work and a small fee, and the only protection a buyer has |
> | **in keeping** | what a temple or guild does with something it holds up for somebody else. *"The Shrine holds three in its keeping."* |
> | **a sliver** | a piece of a life, cut out of somebody and sold. Everybody knows the Low Quay deals in them. Nobody respectable admits to buying one, and the people who sell had run out of other ideas |
> | **short** | what they call a person afterwards. *"She's short now, and she'll be short till she dies."* |
>
> A sliver gets talked about the way people talk about any nasty, legal-ish trade: quietly, and with a
> change of subject.

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **Arcana** and **Insight**
> *Stock* is a bench word. Timber and rope both come in stock, and a sliver is what comes off a piece of
> it under a blade. So a dealer talking about the stock a woman has on her is using the word for
> inventory. Shrine Hill and the Guild say *measure* for the same thing and would never say *stock*.
>
> A clerk writes *lifeforce* where the street says breath. Nobody says that aloud, any more than they say
> *indenture-referral threshold* in a kitchen.
>
> A proving tells you three things: whether there is breath in the piece, roughly how much, and whether
> whoever put it there is still alive. The last one is what you are paying for.
>
> *"He's carrying more sword than he's got breath for"* is a sergeant's phrase for a man using something
> too fine for him. Usually it means a fool. Occasionally it means something worse.

> [!warning]- 🟣 RARE — **expertise in both Arcana and Insight**
> The dealers have no softer word for what they sell. They say *sliver* across a bar in the same voice
> they use for the weather. Nobody polite has a word for it at all, and on paper Records writes
> *unattributed*, meaning nobody can say whose it was.
>
> Here is the part that explains the Low Quay. A person can rent out a piece of their life honestly and
> legally, and almost nobody will pay them for it, because a promise from a dockhand is worth nothing to
> anybody. The dealers pay properly, at once, no questions. The honest trade is not competing with them.
> It is being outbid.
