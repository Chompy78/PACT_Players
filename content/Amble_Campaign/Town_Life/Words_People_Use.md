Stoneharbour has its own words for things, and which one a person reaches for tells you where they drink.
None of this is secret. All of it is worth knowing before somebody uses it at you.

**Three tiers of knowledge.** Read the one your character has — see
[[What_Your_Character_Knows|What Your Character Knows]].

> [!info]- ⚪ COMMON — no proficiency needed
> **Stock** — what a person has made of themselves. Everything they've learned and can do. *"Good stock
> in that one."* Politer people say **measure**.
>
> **Breath** — what a person can hold up in the world: enchanted things, mostly. *"There's a breath of
> Dorn's in that lamp."* Politer people say **bearing**.
>
> **A binder** — the trade that puts breath into objects. Five of them in a town this size, and every one
> of them charges more than you think is reasonable.
>
> **A brewer** — potions and draughts. Usually the apothecary, and usually somebody's aunt.
>
> **A mark** — a binder's stamp on his own work. A good mark is worth more than the object under it.
>
> **A proving** — having a second binder test a piece to see whether there's anything really in it. An
> evening's work, a small fee, and **the only protection a buyer has** in a town where half the charms
> sold at market do nothing at all.
>
> **In keeping** — what a temple or a guild does with something it holds up on somebody else's behalf.
> *"The Shrine holds three in its keeping."*
>
> **A sliver** — a piece of a life, cut out of somebody and sold. Everybody knows the Low Quay deals in
> them. Nobody respectable admits to buying one, and the people who sell are, without exception, people
> who had run out of other ideas. **It is spoken of the way people speak of any nasty, legal-ish trade:
> quietly, and with a change of subject.**
>
> **Short** — what they call a person afterwards. *"She's short now, and she'll be short till she dies."*

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **Arcana** and **Insight**, or **expertise in Arcana**
> **The register split is real and worth hearing.** *Stock* is a bench word — timber and rope both come in
> stock, and a sliver is literally what comes off a piece of it under a blade. **A dealer who says a woman
> has stock on her has called a living person inventory, and nobody in the room notices.** Shrine Hill and
> the Guild say *measure* for the same thing and would never say *stock*.
>
> **A clerk writes _lifeforce_** where the street says breath, and nobody says that aloud any more than
> they say *indenture-referral threshold* in a kitchen.
>
> **What a proving actually tells you**: whether there is breath in the piece, roughly how much, and
> whether whoever put it there is still alive. **That last one is the part worth paying for.**
>
> **"He's carrying more sword than he's got breath for"** — a sergeant's phrase, meaning a man is using
> something too fine for him. It is usually said about a fool, and occasionally about something worse.

> [!warning]- 🟣 RARE — **expertise in both Arcana and Insight**
> **The dealers have no softer word for what they sell.** They say *sliver* across a bar in the same voice
> they use for the weather, and that is worse than a euphemism would be — they are not embarrassed enough
> to have invented one.
>
> **Nobody polite has a word for it at all.** On paper, Records writes *unattributed* — meaning nobody can
> say whose it was, and nobody ever will.
>
> **And the fact that explains the Low Quay better than anything else on this wiki:** a person can rent
> out a piece of their life honestly and legally, and almost nobody will pay them for it, because a
> promise from a dockhand is worth nothing to anybody. **The dealers pay properly, at once, no questions.
> The honest trade does not compete with them. It is outbid by them.**
