The Prelude's sessions, told as connected short stories — one chapter per session, one season each, from
an ordinary spring afternoon to the winter that ended it.

## Chapter 1 — Spring: The Broken Charm

**Chapter 1 summary.** The party are eight years old. A fight breaks out at the council tree over Bram's
grandfather's charm, which Wren had borrowed to draw and then lost out past the old shrine — and on the
way to find it they come across a sheep killed by something that doesn't eat like a wolf. They recover
the charm, and Archer takes it to Bram's door, where his father throws it into the yard rather than thank
a child for it. Then Wren spots something in the woods: a small animal, hurt, glowing faintly blue. A
hunting party is already tracking it, certain it killed the sheep, so Old Marn gives them until nightfall
to prove otherwise. They go back after dark with a stolen lantern and carry it home. It heals. Wren is
voted into the group that evening, under a very serious blood oath none of them understood the rules of.

## Chapter 2 — Summer: Market Day

**Chapter 2 summary.** Four years on and the party are twelve. It opens at Mara's funeral, where Bram's
father disgraces himself and Wren tells Bram exactly what she thinks of a kindness badly aimed. Market
day brings the Iron Coin Guild to Amble for the first time — Marek at a table with a ledger and a line of
desperate people — and then a troll, and a child shoved clear of a bolting horse by
someone who took the animal's shoulder for it. There's a rumour going round that someone has quietly
bought every barrel of lime in the village. That night the party go
looking for rats in the old cart shed and burn it down instead. Every lie they tell the next morning
holds.

## Chapter 3 — Autumn: Who to Believe

**Chapter 3 summary.** The party are fourteen, and Wren doesn't turn up for breakfast. Her sketchbook and
charcoal are found at the edge of the woods, and what's inside the book starts the trail: a cart drawn
over and over, Councilman Pike's house, and a page of four faces — the people who had made her life
hardest. A burial token turns up in her hideout above the shrine that should still be underground with
old Martha. The search splits between the lime pits and the tannery, and it's the tannery that gives them
Halloway: a ledger, a wagon, and a pack of Wren's charcoal in the corner. They find her at dusk, tied to a
shed at the old lime pits, and Bram takes a blade across the face getting to her. The fey creature they
saved when they were eight comes out of the treeline, settles a debt, and goes. In the back of Halloway's
wagon is a body none of them recognise.

## Chapter 4 — Winter: The Iron Coin

**Chapter 4 summary.** Two winters later the party are sixteen and finally allowed inside the tavern.
Mother Yela is found collapsed on the shrine floor under a fine grey dust, and so is Old Corr, the village
healer. Nell rides back from the outlying farms and names it: **Grey Fever**, which turns adults to dust
and mostly spares children. Old Corr dies. The one thing said to cure it is a **Silver Drop**, a magical
pearl — and two Iron Coin Guild men, Marek and Corvin Thale, happen to be carrying one through the
village. The party break into their room, steal it, are chased through the snow, and throw it down the
town well on the strength of a tall tale told by Old Piper, the tavern drunk nobody had ever believed. The
water saves Amble, but not those already gone. The Guild indentures all six of them for seven years and
brands them with an iron coin. Mother Yela dies in the night, and Wren, with nothing left to keep her,
demands to join and leaves with them. In the morning they walk out of Amble for Stoneharbour.
