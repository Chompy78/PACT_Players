You speak **Haldeni**. So does almost everyone you have ever met, and for most people in the South March
that is the end of the subject.

It stops being true the moment you leave the kingdom, and it surprises people.

## The ordinary languages

**Elvish, Dwarvish, Orc, Halfling** and **Gnomish** are all alive and perfectly ordinary. None of them is
exotic and none of them is a relic. Plenty of families keep one up alongside Haldeni the way a family keeps
anything up — because an aunt insisted, because the songs are in it, because it is theirs. Nobody thinks
twice about it.

**Infernal** and **Sylvan** are the same: real languages, known by the people who know them, remarkable to
nobody.

A few places keep something smaller and older than any of those. **Delvern**, up in the mountains, has its
own tongue that is not quite Dwarvish — a hold's own version, from when the hold was its own. Hardly anyone
speaks it now. They still sing one verse of it at a naming or a burial, and most of the people singing could
not tell you what the words mean.

## Down at the harbour

Nobody does business in Haldeni once a ship is involved. They do it in **Trade**.

Trade is nobody's language. It is the thing dockers, crews, carters, factors and customs clerks all use
because it is the only thing everybody on a quay has in common — stripped-down, no grammar worth the name, a
few thousand words that are mostly **Albric**, with bits off whatever the cargo was. You pick it up by
working, not by studying. A season on a quay will do it.

It is superb for a price, a weight, a date, a grade of iron and a threat. *Ten ton, two silver the ton,
Reapmonth, your deck.*

It is useless for anything else, and that matters more than it sounds.

## Past the border

> [!info]- ⚪ COMMON — no proficiency needed
> What everyone down the market will tell you, some of it wrong.
>
> Over in Aeloria the elves talk elvish, only fancier about it — they have a way of making the same
> language sound like they are doing you a favour. Down in Kaldrun they talk dwarvish with a mouthful of
> gravel. So if you have got elvish or dwarvish you will manage well enough in either.
>
> And anyway, everyone who matters speaks Haldeni really. They just make you ask twice.

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **both History and Insight**
> Almost none of that is true, and the two skills together are what show it: History for where these
> languages actually came from, Insight for watching it go wrong in the room.
>
> **Aelvish and Kaldrunic are their own languages.** Aelvish grew out of Elvish and Kaldrunic out of
> Dwarvish, centuries back, and both have drifted far enough to stand alone. Aeloria did it deliberately —
> the chosen caste's tongue, cultivated and formal, and ordinary Elvish is heard there as provincial.
> Kaldrun did it by accident, through mountains and mines and centuries of looking inward.
>
> **So the parent gets you partway and no further.** With Elvish in Aeloria you will follow a price, a
> direction, a number and a threat. You will not follow the law, the liturgy, an argument, or anything said
> quickly. And **you will never pass** — that is not a matter of trying harder, it is a thing a native hears
> inside one sentence. An elf speaking plain Elvish in Aeloria fares worse than a human speaking none at
> all: it reads as one of the chosen who could not be bothered to keep the language.
>
> **Haldeni does not travel the way you have been told.** It goes where the Guild's paperwork goes, because
> the ledgers are written in it, and it stops there. Halden's size bought its language offices, not
> affection.
>
> **And Trade cannot carry a dispute.** You can agree a deal in it and you cannot argue one. When a bargain
> across a border goes wrong, both sides have to fall back into somebody's home language — and whoever's
> language that turns out to be has just won most of the argument before it starts. Worth knowing before
> you shake on anything.
>
> **A thief's cant stops at the border too.** There is no single thieves' cant; every kingdom grew its own.
> Whatever yours buys you at home — a mark read off a doorframe, knowing which table not to sit at — is
> noise abroad.

## If you read the old signs

The old faith of the hills keeps books that are an index of signs, and it keeps the same habit out on the
road. Cut, chalked or scratched onto wayside stones, gateposts, doorframes and lintels, there is a set of
**marks** — a few dozen of them, and no two valleys agreeing on all of them.

They say useful things, never mysterious ones. *This house will feed you and will not ask. This one won't.
Bad water. A keeper two miles on. Don't sleep here. The dog bites.*

Keepers read them. So do almshouse hands, hedge-elders, hedge-druids, and anybody who has been poor enough
and on the road long enough to have been taught. They are not a secret — nobody in this faith keeps secrets
— it is just that almost nobody has ever needed to learn them, so almost nobody has. Everyone else walks
past a scratch on a gatepost.

They are Halden's. Abroad, they are scratches.
