Your characters are seventeen. Almost everybody they meet has been doing their job for longer than your
character has been able to walk.

**Three tiers of knowledge.** Read the one your character has — see
[[What_Your_Character_Knows|What Your Character Knows]].

> [!info]- ⚪ COMMON — no proficiency needed
> **A tradesman is not "an NPC with a hammer". He is the best in the room at his own thing, and usually
> at three other things nobody mentions.**
>
> **A master smith** has thirty years at the anvil — but also **a lifetime of arguing about price**. He
> reads customers for a living. He knows what iron costs this month, what you paid last time, and what
> you are trying not to say. **You will not out-haggle him** and you will not impress him by knowing the
> word for a tool.
>
> **A sailor** has been up a mast in weather since he was twelve. He can climb anything, swim in a sea
> that would kill you, tie a knot you cannot undo, and has eaten worse than you have ever seen without
> being ill. He is not a fighter. **He will still put a seventeen-year-old over a rail without setting
> down his drink.**
>
> **A town guardsman** has spent seven years being hit by people who did not want to be arrested. He is
> in armour, he is not alone, and he has done this on a thousand worse nights than yours.
>
> **A shopkeeper**, a cook, a net-mender, a clerk — every one of them is *expert* at the thing they do
> all day, and most of them are quietly good at reading people, because that is what keeps a small
> business alive.
>
> **And a Guild factor is not a clerk who got promoted.** The Guild does not send bookkeepers to run
> branches in port towns. Whoever holds that desk was chosen for being able to handle what a port town
> produces, and most of them did something else first. **Being courteous is not the same as being
> harmless**, and the ones who never raise their voice have usually never needed to.
>
> **The useful habit: assume the person in front of you is better at their own life than you are.** Ask
> them things. It costs nothing and they know an astonishing amount.

> [!tip]- 🔵 TRAINED — proficient in **Insight** and **History**, or **expertise in Insight**
> **The rough ladder, in the same AP your own character is built with:**
>
> | Who | About |
> |---|---:|
> | **Your party, right now** | **~103 each** |
> | A young deckhand, a couple of years in | 44 |
> | A watchman of two years | 56 |
> | A veteran merchant guard | 143 |
> | **A sailor of twelve years, at 28** | **170** |
> | A Crown regular at Kingshold | 171 |
> | **A town guardsman of seven years, at 28** | **178** |
> | **A master smith** | **221** |
> | An elite King's guard | 284 |
> | A landed knight | 327 |
>
> **Read that carefully.** A sailor who has never been in a real fight is worth more than any of you. A
> town guardsman is worth nearly two of you and is wearing armour. **The master smith you are haggling
> with is more capable than the Crown regular on the gate.**
>
> **What those numbers are made of is not all combat**, which is the part people miss. The sailor's total
> is mostly expertise in climbing, swimming and endurance. The smith's is mostly his craft, his trade
> knowledge, his hands, and being very hard to lie to. **They are not fighters. They are simply
> formidable people.**

> [!warning]- 🟣 RARE — **expertise in both Insight and History**
> **Assume you lose any fight you start in Stoneharbour.** That is the honest reading of the table above.
> Not "it will be hard" — assume you lose. The exceptions are a lone drunk, a boy your own age, and
> somebody who is already hurt.
>
> **You cannot tell by looking.** The watchman worth 56 and the guardsman worth 178 wear the same
> uniform, drink in the same room, and are twelve years apart. The quiet man at the end of the bar is
> more likely to be dangerous than the loud one.
>
> **None of this is permanent, and that is the real point.** You gain in a year what a working adult
> gains in five — you are being stretched and they are not. **Within a few years you will pass the town
> guard, the sailor and the Crown regular**, having never drilled a day. The people who scare you now
> will not scare you for long, and some of them already suspect that.
